# Observing Log, Autumn Session

Four nights at the ridge site, all with the 10-inch reflector. Conditions were unusually stable for the season, and the transparency held well past midnight on three of the four nights.

## Conditions

Seeing was rated on the Antoniadi scale at the start and end of each session. Where the two disagreed, the log records the worse of the pair.

> Seeing held at II for most of the second night, which is the best this site has offered since the spring. The limiting magnitude reached 6.1 near zenith once the last of the valley haze settled out.

Transparency was the limiting factor on the first night rather than Seeing. A thin high cirrus never fully cleared, and faint extended objects washed out badly even when point sources stayed sharp.

## Targets

The [Perseus double cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Cluster) resolved cleanly at 60x and was the best view of the session. Both cores filled the field without crowding, and the color contrast in the brighter members was obvious without averted vision.

M31 showed both companions on the second night. The dust lane was visible directly rather than only with averted vision, which at this aperture depends almost entirely on Transparency rather than Seeing.

> The Veil responded far better to the filter than expected. Without it the eastern segment was a suggestion. With it the braided structure was unmistakable at 45x.

A quick pass through [Stellarium](https://stellarium.org) before each session cut the time spent star-hopping roughly in half, mostly by ruling out targets still too low to be worth the walk.

## Notes for Next Session

The ridge site is dark enough that the limiting factor is now the mount rather than the sky. Tracking drift at 200x becomes obvious within about ninety seconds, which rules out most of the planetary work this site would otherwise support.

Seeing this stable is rare enough that the next clear window should go to double stars rather than extended objects.
